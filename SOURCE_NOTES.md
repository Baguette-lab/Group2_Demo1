# Source review — November 9, 2026

The website distinguishes observed population data, conceptual definitions, fictional case details, interpretive song readings, and the group’s support for divorce as an option.

| Source | Verified use and scope |
| --- | --- |
| PSA original dated press release | 2015/2020 marital-status percentages, Table 2; sex composition, Table 3; regional comparison, Table 4. Denominator: household population aged 10+, people rather than couples. The combined category is not divorce alone. The stable PDF replaces a webpage that now resolves to a generic census page. The PDF is dated February 14, 2023; the old web index date was February 16. |
| 2021 YAFS key findings | 85% not yet in a marital union; 12% cohabiting; 3% formally married. Filipino youth aged 15–24; collection August 2021–January 2022. Eli, 25, is outside that age range. Rounded cross-sectional union-status figures, not breakup rates. |
| Kuang (2025) | Eight 2016 focus groups, 54 adults aged 22–62, one urban Quezon City barangay and one rural Hermosa, Bataan barangay. Wedding costs, commitment, and testing relationships are reported themes. Participants did not mention sharing household expenses, convenience, or housing as reasons. The fictional skit’s commutes and shared-cost reasoning are not attributed to the study. |
| OECD SF3.1 | May 2025 indicator defines annual crude marriage/divorce rates per 1,000 people. Replaced a dead legacy URL with the current webfs PDF. This indicator is conceptual context, not a Philippine divorce estimate. |
| UN Statistics Division and PRB | Household/family distinctions, cohabitation, nuptiality, marital status, and crude rate definitions. |
| Mortimer (2022) | Agency, linked lives, and historical time. Minnesota longitudinal study used for concepts, not Philippine policy outcomes. |
| Philippine Family Code and PD 1083 | Legal separation, annulment/nullity, qualifying foreign recognition, and covered Muslim marriages retain their distinct scopes and effects. |
| Republic v. Bayog-Saito | Exact decision date August 17, 2022, G.R. 247297. Foreign divorce and applicable foreign law must be proved in judicial recognition. |
| Senate S.B. 394, 20th Congress | Filing July 10, 2025 verified. The current legislative progress could not be confirmed from the accessible record; the website identifies a filed proposal and does not infer enactment or absence of a later law. |
| England and Wales; Australia | Official legislation/court guidance supports the scoped examples and minimum stages. They do not describe Philippine procedures. |
| UDHR and CEDAW | Equality, consent, and responsibilities are used as international rights principles, distinct from national divorce procedures. Accessible official UK Act and UN treaty PDFs replace harder-to-fetch pages. |
